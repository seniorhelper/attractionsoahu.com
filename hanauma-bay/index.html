Hanauma Bay: Reservations, Fees and How to Plan Your Visit

Updated November 10, 2026 · rules and fees checked against the City and County of Honolulu's official Hanauma Bay page that day

Short answer: Hanauma Bay Nature Preserve is open Wednesday to Sunday and closed every Monday and Tuesday. Visitors who aren't Hawaiʻi residents pay $25 per person (age 13 and up) and should book online; reservations open two days ahead at 7 a.m. Hawaiʻi time and go quickly. Entry runs from 6:45 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., and everyone has to be out by 4 p.m.

Hanauma is a sunken volcanic crater on Oʻahu's southeast tip, about a 25-minute drive from Waikīkī without traffic. The bay is a marine life conservation district, which is why you'll find more fish here than at almost any other beach you can drive to. It is also why the City runs it like a preserve rather than a beach park: capped numbers, a mandatory education stop and two closed days a week so the reef gets a rest.

The City says its schedule can change at short notice, so check the official page the week you go.

How do Hanauma Bay reservations work?

Admission-only reservations are made on the City's PROS booking site. The window opens two days before your visit at 7 a.m. Hawaiʻi Standard Time. To go on a Thursday, you book on Tuesday morning; for a Wednesday visit you book on Monday. Popular early slots can be gone within minutes, so be logged in and ready a few minutes before 7 a.m. Hawaiʻi time. If you're on the mainland, remember Hawaiʻi doesn't use daylight saving time: 7 a.m. HST is 10 a.m. Pacific or 1 p.m. Eastern in winter, an hour later on the clock in summer.

A few rules catch people out. Reservations can't be refunded or transferred. One booking covers at most 10 people (no more than five adults and five children). And a non-resident traveling with a Hawaiʻi resident still pays the visitor rate.

Can I book further ahead?

Yes, if you want transport included. Roberts Hawaiʻi sells admission-plus-transportation packages through hbay.robertshawaii.com up to about a month in advance. It costs more than driving yourself, but it's the simplest way to lock in a date before you fly, and you skip the parking scramble.

What if I didn't get a reservation?

The City holds back roughly a quarter of daily entry for walk-ins, handed out first come, first served when the parking gate opens at 6:45 a.m. Everyone in your group has to be there in person. On weekends and holidays the line forms well before opening, so treat walk-in entry as a backup plan rather than a sure thing.

Planning a good snorkel day

  • Go early. Morning usually brings calmer water, better visibility and a less crowded beach. The latest you can enter is 1:30 p.m., but you'd only get a couple of hours in the water.
  • Stay inside the reef unless you're a strong swimmer. The shallow inner reef is where most people snorkel. Currents outside the reef can be strong, and lifeguards will tell you what the day's conditions are.
  • Don't stand on coral. Much of the inner reef sits close to the surface at low tide. Float, use the sandy channels, and keep your fins up.
  • Don't feed or touch the fish. Feeding was banned in the bay's 1990 management plan, and touching or chasing turtles is against state and federal law.
  • Use mineral sunscreen or a rash guard. Hawaiʻi bans the sale of sunscreens containing oxybenzone and octinoxate; a long-sleeved rash guard means you need less sunscreen in the first place.
  • Bring cash for parking and plenty of water. Gear rental and food are available on site, but they're cheaper if you bring your own.

Is Hanauma Bay worth it?

For first-time snorkelers and families, yes. The water inside the reef is sheltered, the fish are plentiful, and lifeguards are on duty. Confident snorkelers who don't want to deal with booking might prefer to skip it and head to other south and west shore spots on a calm day. Whatever you choose, read the ocean conditions first: Oʻahu's beaches change with the season, and summer and winter swells hit different shores.

Getting there

Hanauma Bay is off the Kalanianaʻole Highway (Route 72), just past Hawaiʻi Kai. The lot is small and fills early, which is another reason to book an early slot. There's no long-term parking along the highway nearby, so if the lot is full you'll need to come back later or use the Roberts Hawaiʻi transport option.

Nearby on the same drive

Combine Hanauma with the coastal lookouts toward Makapuʻu or the Diamond Head summit hike on the way back to Waikīkī. For a longer list of ideas around the island, see things to do on Oʻahu.
