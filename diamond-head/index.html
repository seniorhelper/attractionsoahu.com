Hiking Diamond Head: Reservations, Fees and What the Trail Is Like

Updated November 10, 2026 · checked against Hawaiʻi State Parks (DLNR) pages that day

Short answer: Out-of-state visitors need a reservation to hike Diamond Head (Lēʻahi). Entry is $5 per person and parking is $10 per car, paid online when you book, up to 30 days ahead. The park is open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m.; the last reservation slot is 4 p.m. The summit trail is 0.8 miles each way with 560 feet of climb, and most people need 1.5 to 2 hours round trip.

Diamond Head State Monument is the volcanic tuff cone at the east end of Waikīkī. The hike starts on the crater floor and climbs the inside wall to old military lookouts on the rim, with views over Waikīkī, Honolulu and the south shore. It's short, but it's steep and sunny, and the reservation system means you can't just turn up.

How to book

Reservations open up to 30 days ahead on the Hawaiʻi State Parks site. You choose a time window and pay entry (and parking, if you're driving) online. If you're coming by bus, rideshare or on foot from Waikīkī, you only need the walk-in entry reservation, not parking.

Be on time. State Parks asks you to arrive within the first 30 minutes of your window; if you're later, you can be turned away and late arrivals and no-shows aren't refunded. People without a reservation are turned away at the gate, so don't count on a walk-up spot even on a quiet day. Sunrise and early morning windows go first because they're the coolest time to climb.

What the hike is actually like

The first stretch from the crater floor is a paved path with a gentle grade. It turns into a series of switchbacks up the crater wall, where the footing is uneven and there's little shade. Near the top you go through a lit tunnel about 225 feet long and then up a long flight of stairs (the last tenth of a mile is all steps) before coming out at a 1911 fire-control station on the rim. From the summit you can see the 1917 Diamond Head lighthouse below, Waikīkī's shoreline to the west and Koko Head to the east. In winter, keep an eye out for humpback whales offshore.

It's a workout rather than a technical hike. Most reasonably fit people manage it fine at their own pace; take breaks on the switchbacks and let faster hikers pass. People with heart, knee or breathing problems should think twice about the stairs, and it isn't suitable for strollers or wheelchairs. The crater floor itself, with the visitor kiosk, gift shop and an audio tour, is accessible.

Tips for a better hike

  • Go early. The trail faces the sun and there's almost no shade. Early slots are cooler and less crowded.
  • Carry water. More than you think: at least a liter per person in warm weather.
  • Wear real shoes. Sneakers or trail shoes, not flip-flops. The rock is worn smooth in places.
  • Hat and sunscreen. The summit is fully exposed.
  • Leave the dog at home. Pets aren't allowed except service animals.
  • Have your card ready. Fees are card only.

Getting there

The park entrance is inside the crater, off Diamond Head Road, reached through a tunnel on the Kahala side. From Waikīkī, it's a short drive or rideshare. Several city bus routes run along Diamond Head Road and Monsarrat Avenue, from where it's a steep walk up to the entrance tunnel. Parking inside the crater is limited, which is why a parking reservation matters if you drive.

Pair it with

An early Diamond Head climb fits nicely before a snorkel at Hanauma Bay (on a Wednesday to Sunday, with a separate reservation) or a lazy afternoon in Waikīkī. More ideas: things to do on Oʻahu.
