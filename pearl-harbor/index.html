Visiting Pearl Harbor: USS Arizona Memorial Tickets and Planning

Updated November 10, 2026 · checked against the National Park Service and Recreation.gov that day

Short answer: Entry to Pearl Harbor National Memorial is free. The USS Arizona Memorial boat program is free too, but you should reserve a timed ticket on Recreation.gov, which charges a $1 non-refundable fee per ticket. Tickets open 56 days (8 weeks) ahead at 3 p.m. Hawaiʻi time, with more released at 3 p.m. the day before. Bags aren't allowed inside, and parking is $7 a day.

Pearl Harbor is the most visited historic site in Hawaiʻi, and for many people it's the most moving stop of the trip. The National Park Service runs the visitor center and the USS Arizona Memorial, which sits over the sunken battleship where 1,177 crew members died on December 7, 1941. Other ships and museums around the harbor are run by separate nonprofit organizations.

Pearl Harbor at a glance

Visitor centerOpen daily 7 a.m.–5 p.m. Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. No entrance fee.
USS Arizona Memorial programAbout 45 minutes, including the round-trip boat ride. First program 8 a.m.; last boat 3:30 p.m.
TicketsRecreation.gov, $1 per ticket. Released 56 days ahead at 3 p.m. HST; extra tickets released the day before at 3 p.m. HST. Limited standby on the day.
BagsNo bags of any kind (backpacks, purses, camera bags) in the memorial or visitor center. Paid bag storage near the entrance.
Parking$7 per day, paid by app or QR code in the lot.

How to get USS Arizona Memorial tickets

Reservations are strongly recommended. The rolling window on Recreation.gov opens 56 days in advance at 3 p.m. Hawaiʻi Standard Time, so as soon as you know your dates, book. If your date is already gone, try again at 3 p.m. HST the day before your visit, when a second batch is released. Morning programs are the most popular.

No reservation? A limited number of standby tickets are given out at the visitor center on the day. It can work on a quiet weekday, but it can mean a long wait, so don't make it plan A in peak season.

The ticket covers the boat ride to the memorial and time aboard it. The NPS documentary film isn't part of the ticket; it's shown separately in the visitor center theater, first come, first served, at 15 and 45 minutes past each hour.

The bag rule (read this before you leave the hotel)

This is the rule that trips most people up. You can't take any bag into the memorial or the visitor center: no backpacks, purses, fanny packs, diaper bags or camera bags. Carry your phone, wallet and camera in your pockets, or use the paid bag storage near the entrance. Leaving bags visible in a parked car isn't a great idea either, so the easiest plan is to leave them at the hotel.

The other Pearl Harbor sites

The Battleship Missouri Memorial (where Japan's surrender was signed in 1945), the USS Bowfin Submarine Museum and the Pearl Harbor Aviation Museum are run by separate nonprofits and sell their own tickets. The Missouri and the Aviation Museum are on Ford Island, reached by shuttle bus from the visitor center area. If you want to see more than the Arizona Memorial, plan on most of a day and check each site's own hours and prices before you go.

Planning tips

  • Allow time. Arizona Memorial and visitor center exhibits alone take a half day. With the Missouri or the Aviation Museum, it's a full day.
  • Arrive early for your slot. Leave time for parking, bag storage if needed, and security.
  • Dress respectfully. It's a war grave and a memorial. Swimwear isn't appropriate.
  • Check for closures. The boat program can be cancelled for weather or maintenance; the NPS calendar lists closures.
  • Traffic. From Waikīkī it's around 30 to 45 minutes depending on traffic on the H-1.

Getting there

The visitor center is at 1 Arizona Memorial Place, Honolulu, next to Aloha Stadium and off the Kamehameha Highway. You can drive (paid parking), take a city bus, or book a tour that includes transport. The NPS phone line is (808) 422-3399.
